## 2. The BDHI warhead: clinical and chemical-biology prevalence

**BDHI has never reached a human clinical trial. Its closest clinical
precedent — the parent natural product acivicin, a 3-*chloro*- (not bromo-)
dihydroisoxazole — did reach Phase I/II oncology trials in the 1980s–90s and
showed real but modest activity, undercut by dose-limiting CNS toxicity.**

**The chemistry-biology programme, roughly chronologically:**

1. **Acivicin (parent natural product, 3-chloro-DHI).** Isolated from
   *Streptomyces sviceus*; a glutamine-mimetic covalent inhibitor of glutamine
   amidotransferases, reacting with active-site cysteines by displacement of
   the halogen ("Byun et al. 2023," full text, §Introduction — see below).
   Reached actual human trials: Taylor et al. 1991, *J Clin Oncol*, a Southwest
   Oncology Group Phase II trial in recurrent CNS malignancies (32 patients,
   mostly glioblastoma) — 4/32 objective responses (12%, including one
   complete remission), but **dose-limiting, schedule-dependent CNS toxicity**;
   two patients withdrawn for neurotoxicity (depression, hallucinations).
   PMID [1649269](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1649269/), DOI
   [10.1200/JCO.1991.9.8.1476](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.1991.9.8.1476).
   Reviewed alongside the other glutamine-antagonist amino-acid analogues in
   Ahluwalia et al. 1990, *Pharmacol Ther*, PMID
   [2108451](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2108451/), DOI
   [10.1016/0163-7258(90)90094-i](https://doi.org/10.1016/0163-7258(90)90094-i).
   Acivicin is chloro-, not bromo-substituted; I found no record of a BDHI
   (bromo) analogue itself entering clinical testing.

2. **Transglutaminase 2 (TG2) — the most developed BDHI medicinal-chemistry
   programme, in vivo but preclinical only.** Choi et al. 2005, *Chem Biol*
   (Khosla lab, Stanford), first BDHI-TG2 inhibitors: several compounds showed
   high TG2 specificity (kinh/KI > 2000 min⁻¹M⁻¹) with **essentially no
   reactivity toward glutathione** (k < 1 min⁻¹M⁻¹) — a favorable
   selectivity signature directly relevant to §3 below. In mice: good oral
   bioavailability, short serum half-life, low toxicity, and efficacy
   synergizing with BCNU (carmustine) against glioblastoma xenografts. PMID
   [15850984](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15850984/), DOI
   [10.1016/j.chembiol.2005.02.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2005.02.007).
   Watts et al. 2006, *J Med Chem*, confirmed active-site targeting by SAR and
   found the **5-(S) stereoisomer markedly more active than 5-(R)** — i.e.,
   the warhead-ring stereocenter is potency-determining, not a spectator. PMID
   [17149878](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17149878/), DOI
   [10.1021/jm060839a](https://doi.org/10.1021/jm060839a). Klöck et al. 2014,
   *J Med Chem*, extended potency/selectivity and found significant
   cross-reactivity with the TG1 isoform. PMID
   [25333388](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25333388/), DOI
   [10.1021/jm501145a](https://doi.org/10.1021/jm501145a). None of this
   reached human trials as far as I could find.

3. **GAPDH.** Galbiati et al. 2023, *Eur J Med Chem* — a spirocyclic BDHI
   covalently inactivates human GAPDH faster than koningic acid (one of the
   most potent known hGAPDH inhibitors), with computational work showing
   conformational rigidification is required to stabilize the
   protein-inhibitor complex before the covalent step; the lead compound had
   **negligible reactivity with free thiols** and reduced growth in four
   pancreatic cancer cell lines. PMID
   [37058971](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37058971/), DOI
   [10.1016/j.ejmech.2023.115286](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejmech.2023.115286).

4. **Byun et al. 2023, *J Am Chem Soc* — the chemoproteomics paper, and the
   one that reports Pin1.** Full details in §4 below; also reports GSTP1,
   HDAC8, GLS, ADA, PGK1, and BTK/BLK engagement. PMID
   [37183434](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37183434/), PMC
   [PMC10719761](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10719761/), DOI
   [10.1021/jacs.3c00598](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.3c00598).

5. **ALDH7A1 (2026, most recent).** Gallo et al. 2026, *J Enzyme Inhib Med
   Chem* — irreversible ALDH7A1 inhibitors from a BDHI-derivative library,
   selective over ALDH4A1 and GAPDH, active against prostate cancer (DU145)
   cells with minimal effect on ALDH7A1-low LNCaP cells or normal fibroblasts.
   Shows the warhead is still an active research area, three years after
   Byun. PMID [42112964](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42112964/), DOI
   [10.1080/14756366.2026.2664708](https://doi.org/10.1080/14756366.2026.2664708).

6. **Two reviews**, useful as a general orientation to the class: Pinto et al.
   2015, *ChemMedChem*, "Inspired by Nature: The 3-Halo-4,5-dihydroisoxazole
   Moiety as a Novel Molecular Warhead for the Design of Covalent Inhibitors,"
   PMID [26607551](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26607551/), DOI
   [10.1002/cmdc.201500496](https://doi.org/10.1002/cmdc.201500496); and Zana
   et al. 2021, *ChemistrySelect*, "Synthesis and Reactivity of
   3-Halo-4,5-dihydroisoxazoles: An Overview," DOI
   [10.1002/slct.202101779](https://doi.org/10.1002/slct.202101779) (found via
   web search, not PubMed-indexed — ChemistrySelect is not always in PubMed's
   scope; I could not independently pull its PMID and am citing the DOI only).

**Order of magnitude: a handful of programmes (TG2, GAPDH, GSTP1, Pin1, HDAC8,
ADA, BTK/BLK, ALDH7A1, GLS), none of which has produced a clinical-stage
compound; the closest clinically-tested relative is the chloro- (not bromo-)
parent natural product, and it failed on CNS toxicity, not covalent chemistry.**

**Reactivity profile and mechanism — does the literature agree with D0067's
correction?**

**Yes, unambiguously.** Byun et al. 2023 report DFT calculations (full text,
§"Computational Analysis Guides the Tuning of BDHI Reactivity") on the
reaction of BDHI with methanethiol: reaction with the **neutral thiol** has a
large transition-state barrier (44–46 kcal/mol, consistent with BDHI's
observed lack of reactivity toward glutathione at physiological pH), while
**nucleophilic addition of the thiolate anion (MeS⁻) to the C3 imino carbon**
is spontaneous and low-barrier (ΔG‡ ≈ 4–8 kcal/mol from the reactant complex).
They explicitly describe the C3 carbon (the imine carbon of the ring C=N,
directly bonded to Br) as carrying a **positive electrostatic potential**,
i.e., an electrophilic sp2 centre that a thiolate adds to — this is
**nucleophilic addition–elimination at an sp2 carbon**, not a backside SN2
displacement at an sp3 centre. The acivicin literature the paper cites
describes the same class mechanism for the parent compound: "the 3-halo-4,5-
dihydroisoxazole scaffold of acivicin can react with cysteine residues... to
form a covalent bond by displacement of the [halogen] atom" via addition to
the ring, not backside attack. **This corroborates D0067's fix** — mapping
`sn2_ring_opening` to `perpendicular_to_plane` geometry rather than backside
SN2 is the chemically correct read of this literature, not merely a fit to
this project's own docking angles.

**Reactivity/selectivity data relevant to ranking:**

- BDHI is **tunable and comparatively selective**: gel-based competitive
  labeling in Jurkat lysates showed BDHI fragments blocking a much more
  restricted set of iodoacetamide-TAMRA-protein interactions than the
  haloacetamide "scout fragments" KB02/KB03.
- Quantitatively, IA-desthiobiotin chemoproteomic profiling found **237, 35,
  9, 0, and 17 liganded cysteine sites** (>75% signal reduction) for five
  different BDHI-fragment substituents, versus **2209 and 3306 sites** for the
  haloacetamide controls KB02/KB03 — roughly 1–2 orders of magnitude fewer
  off-target engagements than haloacetamide, but **not negligible** (237 sites
  is still substantial global engagement for the most reactive substituent
  tested).
- No IC50/Ki was reported against Pin1 (see §4); against GSTP1 the same
  compound (compound **18**) gave IC50 = 15 µM by BDNB-GSH transfer assay —
  i.e., **fragment-level, double-digit-micromolar potency**, not a lead-like
  affinity.

## 3. Liabilities

A med chemist looking at this specific candidate would flag, in rough order of
how load-bearing each is:

1. **The C–Br bond and the bromide leaving group.** This is the reactive
   handle by design, but it is also, mechanistically, a metabolic liability in
   its own right if the molecule is ever exposed to nucleophiles other than
   the target cysteine: any off-target thiol, amine, or even water under
   forcing conditions is a candidate to trigger the same addition–elimination.
   The Byun et al. 2023 GSH-incubation data (§2) is reassuring at
   physiological pH — three tested BDHI analogues formed **no detectable GSH
   adduct in 4 h at pH 7.4**, versus >50% adduct formation for
   chloroacetamide controls in the same window — but that result is
   compound-specific (it depends on 5-substitution) and was not measured for
   *this* candidate. It should not be assumed to transfer without testing.

2. **Hydrolytic/solution stability is substitution-dependent and not
   guaranteed.** Byun et al. explicitly report that one of their BDHI
   analogues — the one bearing a bis(trifluoromethyl)phenyl group — was
   **found to be unstable in phosphate buffer** and was excluded from further
   study for that reason. This is a documented failure mode for the class,
   not a hypothetical one: some 5-substitutions destabilize the ring in
   aqueous buffer even before any protein is involved. This candidate's own
   hydrolytic stability has not been literature-validated (nor, per the anchor
   table, computationally cross-checked against a real Cys113 structure).

3. **Off-target promiscuity is lower than haloacetamide but real, and highly
   substituent-dependent.** See the 237/35/9/0/17-site range in §2 — a factor
   of ~10 spread depending on which fragment is attached, meaning this
   property does not transfer cleanly from the literature's tested compounds
   to this specific R-group combination.

4. **No PAINS/frequent-hitter flag found in the literature I could search**,
   but I could not run this candidate through an actual PAINS/Brenk substructure
   filter in this session (that is Blacksmith's tooling, not Bookworm's) — I am
   reporting an absence of a *literature* PAINS designation for the BDHI class
   generally, not a computed clearance for this molecule. The isoxazole and
   sulfone motifs in the binding element are common and unremarkable
   functional groups; I found no reports flagging *either* the BDHI warhead
   class or the sulfolane-isoxazolylmethylamine scaffold as a promiscuous
   assay artefact class, but this is a "did not find," not a "confirmed
   negative."

5. **The warhead-ring stereocenter (C5, the carbon attached to the amine
   nitrogen) is left unspecified in this candidate's SMILES** (`C2CC(Br)=NO2`
   has no `@`/`@@` marker on that atom, whereas the sulfolane-ring carbon does
   carry a defined `[C@@H]`). Watts et al. 2006 (§2) found a **~50-fold**
   potency difference between the 5-(S) and 5-(R) BDHI stereoisomers on TG2,
   and the Byun et al. 2023 compounds were tested **as racemates** precisely
   because enantiopure synthesis of BDHI is nontrivial. If this candidate's
   ranking or any downstream synthesis assumes a single enantiomer at that
   ring carbon, that assumption is not supported by the SMILES as given, and
   the literature says the choice is not cosmetic.

6. **Metabolic soft spots beyond the warhead itself**: the isoxazole ring
   (aromatic heterocycle, N–O bond) and the sulfone are both common,
   generally metabolically robust motifs individually; I found nothing in the
   literature searched here specific to metabolic liabilities of the
   *combination* used in this candidate (sulfolane sulfone + benzylic
   aminomethyl-isoxazole linker), and did not find any ADME/DMPK data for any
   BDHI compound beyond the TG2 programme's mouse PK note (short serum
   half-life, good oral bioavailability, low toxicity in that specific
   chemotype — not this one).

**Overall:** the literature's own account of BDHI is reassuring on GSH
reactivity and relative (not absolute) selectivity, but it is explicit that
(a) both properties are 5-substitution-dependent and have failed for specific
analogues (buffer instability, cross-isoform reactivity on TG1), and (b) the
ring stereocenter matters a great deal and this candidate's SMILES does not
resolve it.

## 4. The Pin1 BDHI precedent specifically

`data/reference/pin1_covalent_cys113_anchors_2.csv` lists anchor #7,
`Byun-2023-BDHI-fragment`, with `smiles_status = UNVERIFIED`,
`potency_kinetics = UNVERIFIED`, and `promiscuity_flag = n`, citing "Byun 2023,
JACS, 10.1021/jacs.3c00598." I retrieved and read the full text of this paper
(PMID [37183434](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37183434/), PMC
[PMC10719761](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10719761/), DOI
[10.1021/jacs.3c00598](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.3c00598)). Here is exactly
what was, and was not, shown.

**What was shown, specifically for Pin1:**

- The active compound is numbered **compound 18** in the paper — described as
  a **5-phenyl-substituted BDHI analogue** bearing a 4-methoxyphenyl group on
  the recognition element. This is a different (and much smaller/simpler)
  binding scaffold than this project's sulfolane-based candidate; the only
  thing shared is the BDHI warhead itself. I could not extract a full SMILES
  from the article text or its PMC rendering (compound structures live in the
  figures/SI, which are not captured by the plain-text extraction tool
  available in this session).
- **Gel-based assay** (recombinant Pin1 + IA-TAMRA competition): compound 18
  inhibits Pin1 labeling by IA-TAMRA **in a dose-dependent manner** — a
  competition/engagement readout, not a binding constant.
- **PPIase assay** (chymotrypsin-coupled peptidyl-prolyl isomerization assay):
  compound 18 inhibited Pin1's catalytic activity **at 1 and 10 µM after 14 h
  of incubation at 4 °C**. No IC50, no Ki, no time-course kinetics (no kinact/KI)
  were reported for Pin1 — this is qualitative-to-semiquantitative, **fragment-
  level** evidence of inhibition at two concentrations, not a potency number.
- **LC-MS/MS**: the catalytic residue **Cys113 was validated as the site of
  labeling** by mass spectrometry — this is real, direct evidence of covalent
  engagement at the correct residue, and is the strongest single piece of
  data in the paper for Pin1.
- **No crystal structure of the Pin1-BDHI complex exists.** The paper states
  they "employed in silico modeling to predict the potential binding mode" and
  compared the **lowest-energy docked pose** of compound 18 to Sulfopin's
  known binding mode, finding the BDHI warhead could plausibly engage C113 via
  hydrogen bonds to S115, with the 4-methoxyphenyl group occupying roughly the
  same hydrophobic pocket (M130/Q131/T152/H157) that Sulfopin's sulfolane ring
  occupies. **This is a computational/docking claim, not an experimental
  structure.**
- By contrast, for their **second** validated target, GSTP1, they *did*
  attempt crystallography (compound 18 soaked into GSTP1 crystals) — and even
  there, "the position of [compound 18] was **not unambiguously
  determinable**"; they inferred binding indirectly from loss of electron
  density in the α2 helix and mass spectrometry on the soaked crystals, not
  from a resolved ligand density. So even the one target in this paper where
  they *tried* crystallography did not yield an unambiguous covalent-adduct
  structure — Pin1 fared worse than that (no attempt reported at all beyond
  docking).

**Cross-checks for a deposited structure, independent of the paper's own
claims:**

- **PubChem**: no CIDs cross-referenced to this PMID (`xref/PubMedID`
  returned "No CIDs found") — compound 18's structure was not independently
  deposited to PubChem under this citation.
- **RCSB PDB full-text search** for `"PIN1 dihydroisoxazole"` and for
  `"3-bromo-4,5-dihydroisoxazole PIN1"` both returned **zero entries**. This is
  consistent with — though, given it is a full-text search over deposition
  metadata rather than a ligand-substructure search, not fully dispositive of
  — the project's working assumption that **no PDB structure of a BDHI–Cys113
  Pin1 adduct exists.**
- I did not additionally run a PDB ligand chemical-component substructure
  search for the isolated BDHI moiety (as opposed to full-text search); that
  is a further check worth doing if this needs to be airtight, but the
  full-text result plus the paper's own explicit statement ("we employed in
  silico modeling") should already be treated as sufficient confirmation for
  working purposes.

**Bottom line for #4, matching the concern in the task exactly:** yes, this is
genuinely the *one* warhead class in the ranking with **no crystallographic
positive**. The published "verified Cys113 binder" label in the anchors CSV is
accurate only in the narrow sense that Cys113 covalent labeling was confirmed
by LC-MS/MS — it is **not** backed by a resolved structure, a quantitative
potency number, or even a fully specified SMILES retrievable outside the
paper's SI. `shared/reference_set.py`'s own docstring already says this
explicitly: *"One anchor (Byun 2023 BDHI fragment) still has an SI-only
structure and is gated out until its SMILES is resolved."* I was not able to
resolve that SMILES from the full text or PMC rendering in this session either
— doing so would require pulling the paper's Supporting Information PDF
directly (ACS SI is not part of the PMC/PubMed full-text I had access to), and
I'm flagging that as a specific, actionable next step rather than guessing at
the structure.
